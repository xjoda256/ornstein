Why Sony WH-1000XM4 Are the Best Headphones Ever Made

In a world saturated with audio gear, the Sony WH-1000XM4 stand alone. Released in 2020, they remain the gold standard for wireless noise-canceling headphones—and here's why nothing else comes close.

Noise Cancellation That Erases the World

The XM4s feature Sony's industry-leading HD Noise Cancelling Processor QN1. Flip them on, and the world disappears. Not muffled—gone. The hum of an airplane engine, the drone of a lawnmower, the chatter of a coffee shop—all reduced to a faint whisper. The Adaptive Sound Control learns your routines and adjusts accordingly, but even at its baseline, no other headphone in this class rivals the isolation.

"You don't realize how loud the world is until the XM4s turn it off."

Sound Quality That Punches Above Its Weight

These aren't just noise-canceling headphones that happen to play music—they're genuinely great-sounding headphones. The 40mm drivers deliver a warm, detailed sound signature with deep, punchy bass that doesn't overpower the mids and highs. Sony's DSEE Extreme upscaling engine restores detail lost in compression, making even Spotify streams sound near-lossless.

Want more bass? Use the EQ in the app. Want less? There's an EQ for that too. The XM4s adapt to your taste, not the other way around.

Comfort for the Long Haul

I've worn these for eight-hour flights without a hint of discomfort. The ear cups are plush, the headband distributes weight evenly, and at 254 grams, they're light enough to forget you're wearing them. The clamping force is just right—secure but not tight. Glasses-wearers, rejoice: these won't crush your frames into your skull.

Battery Life That Doesn't Quit

30 hours of playback with noise canceling on. 40 hours with it off. And when you're in a rush, a 10-minute charge via USB-C gives you 5 hours of playback. There is no battery anxiety with the XM4s. They simply outlast you.

Features That Actually Work

The Speak-to-Chat feature automatically pauses your music when you start talking—perfect for ordering coffee or answering a quick question without removing the headphones. The multipoint connection lets you switch between your phone and laptop seamlessly. The touch controls on the right ear cup are responsive and intuitive: swipe for volume, tap to pause, cover the cup to activate Quick Attention mode.

And the case—finally, a case that's actually pocketable in a backpack. Slim, sturdy, and it holds the USB cable and airplane adapter.

The Only Thing the XM5s Did Better

Let's address the elephant in the room: the XM5s exist. They have slightly better ANC and a more modern design. But they can't fold flat, they feel less durable, and the sound signature is more neutral (read: less fun). The XM4s fold up nicely, travel better, and still cost significantly less. The XM5s were an iteration, not a replacement.

The Verdict

The Sony WH-1000XM4s are not perfect—the mic sounds muffled in windy conditions, and the lack of aptX support stings for Android users who care about codecs. But they get the fundamentals right better than any competitor. Noise canceling? Best in class. Sound? Excellent and customizable. Comfort? All-day. Battery? Two full work weeks. All for $248.

Four years after release, the XM4s are still the headphones I recommend to everyone—from commuters to frequent flyers to people who just want to tune out the world. They don't just set the standard. They are the standard.

That is why the Sony WH-1000XM4 are the best headphones ever made.
