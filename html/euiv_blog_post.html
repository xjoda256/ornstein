Why EUIV Is the Greatest Game Ever Made

Paradox Interactive has released a lot of grand strategy games. Crusader Kings has the roleplay. Stellaris has the space. Victoria 3 has the economy. But Europa Universalis IV, with its seventeen expansions and a decade of patches, remains the most complete, most absorbing, and most replayable strategy game ever created. Here's why.

The Stage: The Entire World, 1444–1821

EUIV starts in the twilight of the Middle Ages and ends at the dawn of the industrial era. In between, you can guide any nation on Earth—from the Ottomans to the Inca to a one-province Irish minor—through the Reformation, the Age of Exploration, the rise of absolutism, and the revolutionary era. No other game gives you that sweep of history with that level of detail.

The Systems That Click Together

EUIV is a game of interlocking systems. Trade flows from node to node; controlling the English Channel as a minor nation is a puzzle in itself. Monarch points force tough choices about whether to develop, tech up, or core new land. Ideas let you customize your nation's identity. Religion, diplomacy, colonization, war—every system feeds into every other. You don't learn EUIV. You absorb it.

"EUIV isn't a game about history. It's a game about the forces that shape history."

The Map

The map of EUIV is a masterpiece of information design. Over 5,000 provinces, each with its own trade good, culture, religion, and development. Political borders shift as nations rise and fall. Terrain affects movement and combat. The map isn't just a backdrop—it's the game board, and it tells a story every time you look at it.

Replayability Beyond Reason

I have over 2,000 hours in EUIV. I have never formed Spain. I have never played France. I have never done a world conquest. And yet I keep coming back, because every nation plays differently. Playing a horde is not playing a monarchy is not playing a republic is not playing a theocracy. Add in randomized new world, custom nations, and the countless mods, and you have a game you could play for the rest of your life and still find something new.

The Soundtrack

EUIV's music is extraordinary. "The Voyage" still gives me chills. The way the soundtrack shifts as you enter different eras—from the medieval tones of the early game to the martial drums of the age of revolutions—creates a sense of time passing that few games achieve. Turn the music up and disable everything else. It's worth it.

The Verdict

EUIV is not a perfect game. The AI is exploitable. The endgame lag is notorious. Some DLC mechanics are half-baked. But no other game has kept my attention for a decade. No other game has made me care about the fate of a tiny OPM in Indonesia or the religious composition of a German trade node that I have never seen in real life. EUIV is a history simulator, a strategy puzzle, and a story generator rolled into one.

That is why EUIV is the greatest game ever made.
