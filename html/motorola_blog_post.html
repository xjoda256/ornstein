Why Motorolas Are the Greatest Phones Ever Made

Everyone's chasing the $1,200 slab of glass and titanium. Meanwhile, Motorola has been quietly making the smartest phone on the market. Not the most expensive. Not the most hyped. The smartest. Here's why.

Moto Actions: Still Undefeated

In 2013, Motorola shipped the Moto X with wave-to-wake and chop-for-flashlight. Over a decade later, no other manufacturer has implemented gesture controls that feel as natural. Twist your wrist twice to open the camera. Chop twice for the flashlight. Pick up the phone to silence a call. These aren't gimmicks—they're the fastest way to do common tasks, and once you get used to them, every other phone feels broken.

Near-Stock Android, Actually Updated

Motorola's My UX is basically stock Android with a handful of genuinely useful additions. No bloatware. No duplicate apps. No weird skins that fight Google's design language. And while Samsung is still figuring out how to ship Android updates on time, Motorola has gotten respectable with security patches and OS updates on their higher-end models. You get a clean, fast experience that feels like Google intended.

The Headphone Jack

Yes, it's 2026. And yes, Motorola still puts a 3.5mm headphone jack on most of their phones. No dongle. No Bluetooth latency. No "courage." Just a straightforward port that works with every pair of wired headphones you own. It's not retro. It's practical.

"Removing the headphone jack wasn't innovation. It was a dongle-selling scheme. Motorola never fell for it."

Battery Life That Lasts

Motorola phones routinely pack 5,000mAh batteries into devices that don't look like bricks. My Moto G Power routinely goes three days on a single charge. Not "light usage three days." Real three days—navigation, YouTube, calls, emails, the works. Samsung and Apple have caught up on battery, but Motorola got there years earlier and still leads in the midrange.

Build Quality That Makes Sense

Motorola uses plastic and aluminum where it matters, keeps phones light, and doesn't charge you extra for a glass back that'll shatter the first time you drop it. The water-repellent design (they don't call it waterproof, but it survives rain and splashes) is standard across the lineup. These phones are built for real life, not for a YouTube drop test.

The Razr: Actually Folding

While everyone else was making folding phones that open into a tablet nobody asked for, Motorola revived the Razr—a folding phone that folds into a phone. It fits in your pocket. It closes flat. It has a external screen for quick notifications. The Razr understands that folding phones should be smaller, not bigger. That's Motorola thinking.

The Verdict

Motorola isn't trying to impress you with specs. They're trying to make a phone that works. A phone with a headphone jack, great battery, clean software, and gestures you'll actually use. You can spend twice as much on a phone that does less. Or you can buy a Motorola and wonder why everyone else is overcomplicating it.

That is why Motorolas are the greatest phone ever made.

Chop twice for flashlight.
